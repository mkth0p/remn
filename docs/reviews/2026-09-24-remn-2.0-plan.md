# REMN 2.0: remn.tech for professional incident responders

24 September 2026. Branch `browser-only-mode`. This plan continues the
[browser-only analysis of 22 September](2026-09-22-browser-only-analysis.md) and keeps its
item numbers where an item carries over (1b, 2a, 3e…).

## 1. North star

**remn.tech is the workbench a professional incident responder opens for a client's Windows
and Microsoft 365 case: nothing is uploaded, a 15 GB collection opens with every record
kept, the story of each person and machine comes out in minutes, and the report holds up in
front of the client's counsel.**

Everything in this plan is judged by one question: what does it change for a responder who
opens remn.tech on an engagement?

## 2. The user, and what they need

**Who.**
- Consultants at IR firms.
- MSSP analysts.
- In-house responders.
They work on client evidence under contract, often on a client's or a borrowed machine,
against the clock, and hand their work to a colleague or a client.

**What they use today.**
- Collection: KAPE or Velociraptor.
- Processing: Zimmerman's tools (EvtxECmd, Timeline Explorer), Hayabusa or Chainsaw.
- Timelines: Timesketch where they have the infrastructure.
- Everything else: spreadsheets for tracking, a Word template for the report.
Every step is a separate tool, and the case lives in folders.

**What stops them using remn.tech today.**
1. **The evidence goes to a third-party server.** Engagement letters and client data
   policies forbid it, whatever the server keeps. This alone rules remn.tech out for most
   real engagements.
2. **Real collections do not fit.**
   - The public upload cap is 512 MB.
   - The browser store is comfortable up to a few hundred megabytes.
   - A KAPE collection of a few hosts plus a domain controller is 5 to 15 GB.
3. **The deliverables do not fit their workflow.**
   - The report is an HTML file; theirs is a Word template.
   - The timeline does not go to Timesketch or Timeline Explorer.
   - A colleague cannot take over the case.

**What would make remn.tech the tool they open first.**
- They can use it on client evidence and show the client why: nothing leaves, provably.
- It opens their real collections, all of them, and keeps every record.
- It gets to the story faster than their chain of tools, and they can defend every line.
- What they produce fits the rest of their engagement: report, timeline, indicators,
  exhibits, handover.

## 3. What is new, for the field

**1. Nothing leaves the browser, and the client can check it.**
- Parsing, storage, rules, correlation and the model all run on the analyst's machine.
- The page served is the public commit, and anyone can rebuild it and compare.
- remn.tech can be installed and used offline, in an air-gapped lab too.
No professional DFIR tool runs a full case from a web page without a server or an install.

**2. 15 GB and every record, in a web page.** A collection is stored in columns in the
browser's private file system, and searched and run through the rules with an embedded
DuckDB. Measured on REMN's own output:
- a record takes about 2 KB as the browser stores it today;
- the same record takes about 80 bytes in columns (82 bytes on the 278 real
  EVTX-ATTACK-SAMPLES logs, the raw record kept);
- 15 GB of EVTX (6 to 30 million records) therefore needs about 0.5 to 2.5 GB in the browser.

**3. A report that carries its proof.**
- Each claim points to a record in a hashed file.
- The report ships a manifest.
- A second examiner re-derives every claim from the original files, on a verify page or
  with an independent script.

**4. The story of each person and machine, with its gaps named.**
- One identity across mail, Entra, the Unified Audit Log and Windows.
- Their sessions and process trees, in time order.
- A statement of what the evidence cannot show.

## 4. Principles

1. **The evidence stays in the browser.** Whatever still needs the server (mail formats
   until 2.1) is named per file, asks first, and is kept nowhere.
2. **Every record is kept.** Rules, filters and limits never decide what is stored.
   Anything REMN could not read is listed with its reason.
3. **The analyst decides.** Rules, correlation and models propose.
4. **Every statement points to a record, and every gap is named.**
5. **Measured, not claimed:** parsers, rules and models alike.
6. **One implementation per piece of logic.** A second one exists only to check the first,
   for a set time.
7. **Every release is deployed to remn.tech and useful by itself.**

## 5. The releases

Six releases make 2.0, about ten months for one maintainer working with AI assistants. Each
is deployed to remn.tech. Each is judged by what it lets a responder do there that they could
not do before.

### R1: EVTX never leaves the browser (9 to 10 weeks)

**What a responder can do after it:** open remn.tech, drop the EVTX of a client's hosts, and
work the case with the network showing no request that carries evidence. Up to several GB,
every record kept.

**Built:**
- **A three-week feasibility test first, go or no-go:**
  - the Rust `evtx` crate (the decoder the server uses through pyevtx-rs) compiled to
    WebAssembly and run in the ingest worker;
  - DuckDB-WASM storing in OPFS;
  - both under REMN's content security policy;
  - measured on a generated 15 GB / 15-million-record set in Chrome, Firefox and Safari:
    records per second, memory, storage, query times, and the size of what the first visit
    downloads.
- **Parsing in the browser.** Files are read in 64 KB chunks, one worker per file.
  Flattening is ported to TypeScript from `backend/services/parsers/evtx_parser.py`, with
  row parity against the golden corpus.
- **The columnar event store.**
  - Parquet or a DuckDB file in OPFS, per case, keeping every field and the raw record.
  - IndexedDB keeps the case itself: findings, decisions, notes.
  - Search, counts, facets, the timeline and pivots run as SQL in a DuckDB worker. The SQL
    is ported from the server store (`backend/services/store/queries.py`, `sqlfilter.py`),
    whose behaviour is already pinned by the engine parity fixtures.
- **Per-file capability labels (1a).** "Parsed in this browser", on screen and in the
  report.
- **Completeness ledger for EVTX (1b):**
  - records read and failed;
  - EventRecordID ranges and their holes;
  - chunks that could not be decoded.
- **A data-handling statement a responder can quote to a client.**

**Exit:**
- Playwright asserts zero evidence-bearing requests for an EVTX case on the public build.
- Field parity at least 99.9% against the golden corpus.
- EVTX-ATTACK-SAMPLES gives the same 269 detections through the browser path.
- A 2 GB Security.evtx opens past today's 512 MB cap.
- remn.tech says "EVTX is parsed in your browser", and only that.

### R2: the 15 GB case (6 to 8 weeks)

**What a responder can do after it:** drop a whole KAPE or Velociraptor collection of 10 to
15 GB and have every record searchable and every default rule run.

**Built:**
- **Rules as SQL in the browser.** The server store's rule compiler
  (`backend/services/store/rules.py` and `sqlfilter.py`, about 1,200 lines) is ported to
  TypeScript and runs inside the DuckDB worker. The engine parity fixtures and the attack
  samples hold it to the JavaScript engine. 2,900 rules over 30 million rows are then
  evaluated a column at a time, not a record at a time.
- **Packages in the browser.** ZIP read as a stream, EVTX members parsed locally, and a
  per-member manifest. Members the browser cannot read yet (registry hives) are listed with
  the reason, or sent to the server only when the analyst allows it for that file.
- **Throughput.** Files parsed in parallel workers, resumable ingest, progress with records
  per second and time left.
- **Existing cases.** A case stored in IndexedDB rows converts to the columnar store when it
  is opened; bundles in format 2 still import.

**Exit, in Chrome on a 16 GB laptop:**
- 15 GB ingested in under 30 minutes.
- A search, count or facet over 15 million rows in under 2 seconds.
- The default rule set in under 10 minutes.
- Nothing lost: records stored equals records read, per file.

### R3: Microsoft 365 in the browser, and a build anyone can check (6 to 8 weeks)

**What a responder can do after it:**
- Work a Microsoft 365 or Entra case with no upload.
- Show a client's security team that the page they ran is the published commit.
- Use remn.tech offline.

**Built:**
- **M365 in the browser.** Unified Audit Log CSV and JSON, and Graph and Entra sign-in
  exports, in TypeScript under golden parity (from `backend/services/parsers/m365.py`), into
  the same columnar store.
- **A reproducible build (6b):**
  - pinned toolchain and fixed timestamps;
  - CI builds each commit twice and compares;
  - remn.tech serves `/.well-known/remn-build.json`;
  - `tools/verify_instance.py` compares the live site with a local build.
- **Installable and offline (6e).**
  - A service worker keeps the app. A new version is installed only when the analyst
    accepts it, never in the middle of a case.
  - The same build can be downloaded as a zip for an air-gapped lab.
- **The server's EVTX and M365 parsers retire from remn.tech.** The public server then
  receives no Windows or Microsoft 365 evidence at all.

**Exit:**
- An EVTX plus M365 case (the lab without its mailbox) makes zero evidence-bearing requests.
- Two CI builds of one commit are byte-identical, and remn.tech passes
  `verify_instance.py`.
- The app loads and works with the network off.

### R4: the defensible record and the deliverables (6 to 8 weeks)

**What a responder can do after it:**
- Hand the client a report whose every claim a second examiner can check.
- Carry the case into their own templates and tools.

**Built:**
- **Record keys everywhere (2a).** Each record is keyed by its file's SHA-256 and a locator
  (channel, EventRecordID and chunk offset for EVTX; record id for M365). Findings,
  decisions, marks and citations point to keys, so a re-ingest or an import never renumbers
  a conclusion.
- **One journal (2b).** The AI ledger (`frontend/src/ai/ledger.ts`) becomes the case journal.
  Every decision, by the analyst or accepted from the model, goes through one `decide()`,
  hash-chained.
- **Exhibits (2d).** Each cited record exports with its file hash, locator and original
  XML.
- **The report manifest and the verify page.**
  - The verify page re-reads the original files and re-derives each cited record and claim.
  - `tools/remn_verify.py` (python-evtx, independent of REMN's parser) checks the exhibits.
- **What the evidence cannot tell (1e).** Missing sources, audit policy gaps, cleared logs
  and record holes, printed in "Where it stops".
- **Deliverables professionals use:**
  - the report as DOCX and Markdown for their templates, next to the HTML;
  - the timeline for Timesketch (JSONL) and Timeline Explorer (CSV);
  - indicators as valid STIX 2.1 and MISP, with analyst verdicts, not every observed value
    (7d).

**Exit:**
- 150 decisions survive a re-ingest and a bundle round trip (property test).
- A second person finds 20 of 20 exhibits with EvtxECmd.
- The verify page passes on the lab report, and fails on a changed report or a changed
  evidence file.
- The STIX export validates, and Timesketch imports the timeline.

### R5: the story, at scale (6 to 8 weeks)

**What a responder can do after it:** click a person or a machine and get their whole story
across mail, Entra, audit records and Windows, with the gaps named, on a 15 GB case.

**Built:**
- **Identity resolver (3b).** It joins:
  - UPN, SAM and domain;
  - SID;
  - mail address;
  - Entra object id;
  - display name.
  Each join has a confidence, and a bare-name match reads "possibly the same".
- **Host lineage (3e).** Process trees and logon sessions (4624 to 4634, RDP hops) built as
  SQL tables once per ingest. AI 2.0 computes them on demand today.
- **Story engine.** Chains (`frontend/src/data/chains.ts`) generalised to any seed: a
  person, a host or a finding. It collects mail, sign-ins, audit records and host activity,
  attaches the findings, and ends with its gaps.
- **Measured detection in the UI (4a, 4b, 4c).**
  - On each rule and finding: fired on N of M samples of its technique, and X benign hits
    per 10,000 background events.
  - Hayabusa findings fused with REMN's own.
  - Rules with no measured sample become hunt signals.
- **Hunting at scale (5b, 5c):**
  - ±N minutes around a record;
  - stacking (least frequent values per host) over 15 million rows.
- **Six areas instead of sixteen views:** Intake, Triage, Stories, Hunt, Decide, Report.

**Exit:**
- The lab reads to 9 of 9 stories, one incident per person.
- S04 shows the session, the hop and the process tree.
- With Sysmon.evtx removed, "what ran" reads "limited to 4688", and the report says so.
- Stacking over 15 million rows in under 5 seconds.
- No default rule without a measured sample.

### R6 (2.0): handover, and a co-investigator on the analyst's machine (6 weeks)

**What a responder can do after it:**
- Pass a case to a colleague or a second examiner through any channel.
- Let a local model work the case beside them.

**Built:**
- **Encrypted case bundles.** WebCrypto X25519 plus AES-GCM, or a passphrase. The bundle
  carries the case state; the columnar rows travel as Parquet inside it.
- **Second-examiner mode.** Each decision shown to confirm or dispute, disputes in the
  journal. A merge of two copies of a case (the journal is append-only, so the merge is a
  union).
- **The co-investigator (AI 2.0 continued).**
  - Triage when evidence finishes, proposals only.
  - Claims checked against the rows they cite, not only that the rows exist.
  - Drafted "what happened" and answer sheet (2f).
  - Guided local model setup, with a check that tells the analyst which model on their
    hardware holds up.
  - REMN-Bench: the lab's stories and injection suite run against local models, with the
    results published.

**Exit:**
- A case passes from one browser to another, is reviewed, and merges back with every
  decision attributed.
- The injection suite changes no decision on any benchmarked model.
- At least five local models are benchmarked and published.

### After 2.0

- **2.1:**
  - EML and mbox parsed in the browser, with the mail analysis and risk score ported under
    parity; PST, OST and MSG stay on the labelled server tier;
  - analyst signatures on reports and bundles (Ed25519).
- **Then, one source at a time, on the browser parser framework, each with golden files:**
  CloudTrail, Okta, Google Workspace, Linux journald and auditd.

## 6. Running remn.tech

- **What the server does after R3:**
  - serves the static app and the build manifest;
  - parses only the mail formats the browser cannot read yet, per file, with the analyst's
    consent;
  - keeps nothing.
  The parse container can then be switched off entirely on an instance that does not
  accept mail.
- **Cost and uptime.** After R3, remn.tech is mostly static files, so hosting is cheap and a
  parser outage no longer stops Windows or M365 work.
- **Updates.**
  - Every release is a tagged commit with a published build digest.
  - The installed app updates only when the analyst accepts.
  - The release notes say what changed in parsing and rules, because that can change a
    conclusion.
- **Observability without evidence.**
  - Counters of requests and errors at Caddy, no bodies.
  - Health shows the build.
  - Abuse handling stays as today: rate limits, size caps.
- **Validation with real users.** Before R4 settles the deliverables, three to five working
  IR analysts try remn.tech on a real or scrubbed case, and their report and handover needs
  set the formats. After each release, the same group says what stops them using it on the
  next engagement.

## 7. Architecture

**What changes:**
- **Parsing moves into the browser:** TypeScript, plus the `evtx` crate as WebAssembly.
  - Python parsing remains for PST, OST, MSG and registry hives only.
  - The server's EVTX and M365 parsers retire on remn.tech after R3, and on self-hosted
    servers one release later.
- **Events are stored in columns in OPFS,** queried by DuckDB-WASM in a worker. IndexedDB
  keeps the case state. The server store (`backend/services/store/`) stays for
  self-hosted installs that prefer it, fed by the same browser parser.
- **One SQL layer.** The server store's queries and rule compiler are ported to TypeScript.
  The JavaScript rule engine stays as the reference that checks the SQL, through the
  existing parity fixtures.
- **Workers:**
  - ingest (one per file);
  - DuckDB;
  - rules;
  - stories and lineage;
  - claims (R6).
- **Record keys and the journal (R4).** Dexie version 7. Existing rows are back-filled
  where their keys can be computed; the rest of a case is labelled "pre-2.0". Bundle
  format 3 imports format 2.

**Browsers:**
- Chrome and Edge fully supported.
- Firefox supported once the R1 test confirms OPFS and DuckDB-WASM on it.
- Safari for evaluation only: it can clear a site's storage after 7 days without a visit.

**What stays:**
- The rule language and its engines, and the parity fixtures.
- The approval inbox, where the model only proposes.
- The public deployment's hardening.
- The case bundle as the unit of exchange.

**Not built:**
- Pyodide, and anything else that ships Python to the browser.
- A graph canvas before lineage tables exist.
- Live multi-analyst editing.

## 8. What 2.0 will not do

- **No evidence on the server:** no feature that parses, stores or analyses Windows or M365
  evidence on remn.tech after R3.
- **No cloud models, no API keys, no WebGPU.**
- **No new platforms before 2.0** (Linux, macOS, AWS, Okta and so on). REMN reads KAPE,
  Velociraptor and Zimmerman output correctly instead.
- **Not a SIEM:** no streaming ingest, no alerting.
- **No rule counts as a claim.** Measured coverage replaces them.
- **What REMN stops doing:**
  - adding top-level views;
  - server-only analysis endpoints;
  - exporting every observed value as an indicator;
  - the honeypot in this repository.

## 10. Risks

- **The browser cannot hold 15 GB on some machines.**
  - The R1 test measures it in each browser.
  - The fallback is the same app installed on the analyst's machine with the server store,
    so the evidence still stays local.
- **DuckDB-WASM limits.** A 4 GB WebAssembly memory ceiling per instance; queries that
  aggregate high-cardinality columns are the risk. Mitigated by column-at-a-time SQL,
  bounded result sizes and the R1 measurements.
- **First visit weight.** DuckDB-WASM and the decoder add tens of MB; they load when the
  first file is dropped and stay cached.
- **Two parsers during the move.** The parity harness holds them together until the
  server's EVTX and M365 paths retire on a set date.
- **One maintainer, ten months.**
  - Each release ships alone.
  - The "will not do" list holds.
  - The pilot group keeps the work on what professionals actually need.
- **A verify page that trusts REMN.** The independent script and a documented manifest
  format make a third check possible.
- **Licences.** No GPL or AGPL code goes to the browser; dissect and extract-msg stay on
  the server tier. REMN stays Apache-2.0.

## 11. Decisions

**Taken on 24 September 2026:**
- **User and first win.** remn.tech is built for professional IR analysts, and the first
  thing it guarantees is that their evidence never leaves the browser.
- **Scope.** Depth on Windows, Microsoft 365 and mail, no new platform before 2.0.
- **EVTX decoding.** The Rust `evtx` crate compiled to WebAssembly.
- **Mail.** EML and mbox in the browser in 2.1; PST, OST and MSG stay on the server tier.
- **Size and completeness.** Cases up to 10 to 15 GB of evidence, with every record kept,
  hence the columnar store in OPFS and SQL rules in the browser.

**Still open, each before the release that needs it:**
1. **R3:** remn.tech's server after R3: mail only, with consent per file (recommended), or
   switched off entirely until mail moves into the browser.
2. **R4:** the report formats beside HTML. DOCX and Markdown are recommended; the pilot
   group confirms.
3. **R5:** the six areas replacing the sixteen views (recommended).
4. **R2:** Firefox as fully supported or best effort, after the R1 measurements.
5. **R4:** old cases: migrate where record keys can be computed and label the rest
   (recommended).
6. **Version names:** each release as 0.2 to 0.7 and 2.0 at R6 (recommended), or 1.x
   numbers.
